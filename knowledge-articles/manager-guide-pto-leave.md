# Manager Guide: Approving PTO and Leave

This guide is for Tamarack Outdoor people managers. It explains how to handle your team's PTO requests and leave questions. It is effective January 1, 2026. Please don't share this guide with employees.

## Your role as a manager in PTO and leave

As a manager, you approve or decline PTO requests from your direct reports. You do not approve or decline leaves of absence: HR decides leave eligibility. Your job on leave is to point the employee to Tamarack HR Hub, keep what they tell you private and plan coverage.

## Approving PTO requests within 3 business days

Managers must approve or decline every PTO request within 3 business days of receiving it in Tamarack HR Hub. Before you decide, check:

- Notice: requests for 3 or more consecutive workdays need 2 weeks' notice. You may still approve a late request if the schedule allows.
- New hires: employees can use PTO only after 60 days of employment. Tamarack HR Hub blocks requests before then.
- Balance: Tamarack HR Hub does not allow a request larger than the employee's available PTO balance.

If you decline a request, add a reason in Tamarack HR Hub and talk with the employee about other dates.

## Store peak period PTO rules (November 20 to December 31)

For store managers, PTO requests for dates from November 20 to December 31 follow the store peak period rule: at most 1 associate off per shift from Nov 20 to Dec 31 unless approved by the district manager. To let a second associate off on the same shift, get your district manager's approval first.

When more requests come in than the rule allows, decide in the order the requests were submitted in Tamarack HR Hub. Encourage your team to request holiday-season time off early. Company holidays when the store is closed (Thanksgiving Day and Christmas Day) don't count toward the limit.

## Viewing your team's PTO balances

Managers can see their direct reports' PTO balances in Tamarack HR Hub under My Team, then Time Off, along with the PTO requests waiting for your approval. You see your direct reports only, not other teams. Balances shown are as of the end of the most recent pay period.

## What managers can and cannot see in Tamarack HR Hub

Managers can see three things about their direct reports in Tamarack HR Hub: job information (title, location, employment type and hire date), schedules and PTO balances.

Managers cannot see a direct report's benefit elections, 401(k) contributions, sick time balance, leave reasons or medical details. If an employee asks you about any of these, send them to Tamarack HR Hub or their HR partner. HR partners can see everyone's records.

## Routing leave of absence requests to HR

When an employee mentions they may need a leave of absence, for their own health, a family member's health or a new child, don't approve, deny or promise anything. Point them to Time Off, then Request a Leave in Tamarack HR Hub, and let them know their HR partner will contact them. If they ask about pay during leave, refer them to HR.

## Keeping medical details private

Never ask about or discuss an employee's medical details, including a diagnosis, condition or treatment, with the employee's coworkers or with anyone else. If an employee shares medical information with you, keep it to yourself and don't write it down in performance notes or messages. If you receive a doctor's note or medical document, send the employee to upload it in Tamarack HR Hub, and don't keep a copy.

An approved leave shows on your team's schedule as time away, without the reason. That is by design.

## Sick time and accommodation requests

Don't require a doctor's note for a sick absence of fewer than 3 consecutive scheduled workdays. If an employee asks for a change at work because of a disability, pregnancy or religious practice, treat it as an accommodation request: route it to their HR partner right away, even if the request is informal.

## Questions about this manager guide

For questions about approving PTO or handling leave, contact your HR partner or email hr@tamarack-outdoor.example.
